Licensing is a frontier we have to survey, and it is far more than a checkbox we tick

Suppose we have found ten datasets and want to assemble them into a training corpus. Each one arrives with a claimed license, and because a license is really an obligation that we take on, a single source with no real provenance is enough to render the whole blend unusable. As we toggle sources in and out, we can read each tier by its color, where green means a permissive source, amber means a source that carries a condition we must honor, and red means a source whose origin is unknown or scraped, and we can watch what even one red source does to the mixture.

Step 3 · audit the claim
A claimed license is only a self-report about a dataset. When the Data Provenance Initiative audited more than 1,800 popular datasets, it found that license information was frequently missing or mislabeled, so it is worth revealing the audited license for these well-known cases.

Provenance is the one thing standing between us and a corpus that we cannot legally ship, which is why it deserves to be treated as real engineering rather than paperwork. The remedy is a mechanical habit of tracking license, source, and audit status at the level of each shard instead of the corpus as a whole, and whenever we cannot name where a token came from we treat it as red.
The 2023–2026 legal reality
NYT v. OpenAI (filed Dec 2023)
This case alleges that millions of articles were used without permission and reproduced almost verbatim, which shows us that scraped news sits squarely inside active litigation rather than in some comfortable grey area.
Books3 taken down (Aug 2023)
Around 196k pirated books inside The Pile were removed after copyright complaints, which reminds us that the mere fact of something appearing in a famous corpus never amounts to a license.
Licensed-data deals
Labs now pay publishers and forums for training rights, so clean provenance carries a real market price, and that price is exactly the reason unknown data becomes a liability on our books.
EU AI Act · GPAI Code (2025)
Providers are now required to publish a "sufficiently detailed summary" of their training content through a Commission template, and once that obligation applies, per-source provenance turns into a legal deliverable that we have to be able to produce.
