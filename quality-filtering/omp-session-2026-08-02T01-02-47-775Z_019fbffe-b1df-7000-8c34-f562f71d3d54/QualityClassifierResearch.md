Teacher→distilled-student labeling. FineWeb-Edu: strong LLM (Llama-3-70B-Instruct) rates a sample 0–5 on an educational-value rubric; a small classifier is distilled from those labels for corpus-scale scoring (Penedo et al. 2024). Adapt the rubric to Hindi with Hindi-specific negatives (code-mixed spam, Romanized menus, OCR garbage).\n\n2. Explicit ordinal rubric, not binary good/bad. FineWeb-Edu's 0–5 scale ties levels to concrete properties, enabling post-hoc threshold tuning rather than baking a cutoff into the labels.\n\n3. Hard-negative mining recipe (DCLM). Train v0 fastText classifier (positives from a trusted corpus, negatives from raw CC) → score a large unlabeled pool → harvest known-negatives that v0 rates as \"good\" (hard negatives) → add to training set → retrain (Li et al. 2024). Most concrete, directly reusable recipe found.\n\n4. Layered pipeline; classifier is one stage, not the whole filter. Gopher/MassiveText and CCNet/Sangraha order: language/script ID → heuristic filters (repetition, symbol ratio, length) → learned quality filter → deduplication (Rae et al. 2021; Wenzek et al. 2019).\n\n5. Perplexity/classifier scores are distributional-similarity proxies, not semantic-quality or factuality measures, and are not portable across languages/tokenizers without recalibration (CCNet, Sangraha). Never reuse an English threshold for Hindi.\n\n6. Threshold selection validated by extrinsic downstream evidence, not classifier accuracy alone. FineWeb-Edu's score≥3 cutoff came from training ~1.8B-param models on ~15B tokens at multiple thresholds and comparing downstream benchmarks — not from classifier F1 in isolation (Penedo et al. 2024).\n\n7. Per-language classifiers outperform reusing one multilingual/English classifier. FineWeb2 trains language-specific classifiers rather than applying English FineWeb-Edu cross-lingually, because rubric interpretation and score calibration differ by language.\n\n8. Stratified failure-mode evaluation is a repeatedly flagged necessity. Sangraha, CulturaX, and Nemotron-CC discussions converge: classifiers commonly penalize Romanized Hindi, code-mixed text, and short-but-useful documents. Aggregate metrics hide this.\n\n## Proposed classifier design (assignment scale)\n\n- Rubric: 0=spam/boilerplate/mislabeled-language, 1=noisy-but-usable, 2=acceptable general text, 3=clearly valuable — (Inference: simplified extrapolation of FineWeb-Edu's scale, not itself sourced).\n- Model: fastText for the deployed filter (matches DCLM/CCNet precedent, tractable at small scale); a small transformer (e.g., IndicBERT-class) as teacher/secondary check — (Inference for the teacher choice).\n- Splits: stratify by script × domain × label before splitting; hold out entire sources from test to prevent template leakage — (Inference, generalized from dedup/leakage concerns in Gopher/DCLM/CCNet).\n- Hard-negative mining: follow DCLM's loop exactly (source-supported).\n- Calibration/threshold: report full precision-recall curves + per-slice (script/code-mix/domain) breakdown; set threshold against intended-use metric, not bare accuracy — (partially inferred: extends FineWeb-Edu's ablation logic to a smaller compute budget).\n- Audit metrics: precision/recall/F1 per rubric level and per script-stratum; calibration plot (score vs. human label); false-positive rate specifically on Romanized/code-mixed Hindi; duplicate-content rate in retained set.\n\n## Minimum evidence standard (avoids synthetic-control claims passing as generalization)\n\n1. Locked, human-labeled Hindi test set never touched during hard-negative mining or threshold tuning.\n2. Metrics reported per stratum (script/code-mix/domain/length), not only aggregate.\n3. Threshold justified against a use-tied metric computed on the locked test set, with tuning kept separate.\n4. Explicitly distinguish internal classifier metrics (accuracy/F1 on a held-out split of the same labeling process) from extrinsic validation (e.g., downstream LM-loss/task improvement, as FineWeb-Edu did). Absent any extrinsic check, label the result a synthetic/proxy result — not generalization.\n5.
