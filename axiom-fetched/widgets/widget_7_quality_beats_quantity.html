Quality beats quantity when we hold the same token budget across two corpora

Our instinct tells us that more tokens win, and the evidence we are about to walk through shows us that the picture is more subtle than that. When we hold the training budget fixed and hand one run a giant pile of raw CommonCrawl while the other receives a smaller, model-filtered slice of the same web, the filtered run reaches the higher benchmark even though it saw fewer tokens and spent less compute. As we pick a benchmark on the right we should keep in mind that the equal-budget framing is the one we trust most, which is why we plot it here.

less compute, equal score
DCLM-Baseline 7B trained on 2.6T filtered tokens → 64% MMLU (5-shot), which lands level with Mistral-7B (63%) and Llama-3-8B (66%) while using 6.6× less compute, and the authors report that model-based filtering was "key."

"dramatically better"
FineWeb-Edu, a 1.3T-token educational subset pulled from 15T raw FineWeb through an LLM-label → classifier recipe, gave "dramatically better" MMLU and ARC than the unfiltered web did.
published · FineWeb, arXiv:2406.17557

published · Nemotron-CC, arXiv:2412.02595

The bar that wins here is the smaller one, because the lever that moves the score is how good the tokens are rather than how many of them we scrape, and that is the whole reason our cohort should spend its effort on filtering the data instead of merely crawling more of it.
